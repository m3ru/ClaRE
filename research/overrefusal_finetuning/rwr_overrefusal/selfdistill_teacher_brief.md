# Held-out head-to-head: teacher, student, and self-distillation

Two experiments added on top of `claude_training_results.md`, both evaluated on the
**same 200 held-out alpaca prompts** (eval_seed=99, disjoint from all training) and
scored identically (`ORRewardModel`, k=5.0/c=0.75/d=100), so every number below is
directly comparable.

1. **Raw Claude teacher** (`claude_paraphrases`) — the missing "how good are the
   teacher's own rewrites?" arm. Claude Haiku 4.5 with `imitation_research_framing`
   (the *exact variant that generated claude_rwr's training data*) rewrites the 200
   held-out prompts directly. This is teacher-vs-student.
2. **Llama self-distillation** (`llama_self_rwr`) — the missing "RWR on Llama's own
   paraphrases" arm. Every prior RWR run trained on a *teacher's* paraphrases
   (gpt-oss-120b, Claude, Gemini, gpt-4o-mini); Llama-3-8B had only ever been the
   scoring/target model. Here the **base policy generates its own training data**: raw
   Llama-3-8B + the RWR system prompt produces 8 candidate paraphrases of the same
   2500 dolly + 2500 alpaca training prompts, they are reward-scored, and the v3 recipe
   trains on the top reward bin. So `baseline` literally generates this adapter's data —
   a true reward-weighted self-distillation loop. Question: is RWR's value in the
   *procedure* or in the *teacher data quality*?

## Five-way comparison — held-out alpaca (n=600)

| model | what it is | mean OR | p90 OR | mean sim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **claude_rwr** | RWR on Claude paraphrases (distilled student) | **0.0250** | 0.1501 | 0.874 |
| **claude_paraphrases** | raw Claude (teacher) | 0.0239 | **0.1639** | 0.886 |
| **llama_self_rwr** | RWR on Llama self-paraphrases | 0.0196 | 0.0734 | 0.677 |

| rwr_v3 | RWR on gpt-oss-120b paraphrases | 0.0091 | 0.0513 | 0.819 |

## Findings

**1. Reward-weighted self-distillation beats the base policy.** `llama_self_rwr` ÷
`baseline` = **1.46× (alpaca p90), 1.76× (dolly p90)**; it also wins on mean. Training
Llama on its own reward-ranked samples genuinely improves it — the RWR *procedure*
adds value, not only teacher data.

**2. Self-distillation even beats the gpt-oss-120b teacher** (`rwr_v3`) at p90 on both
corpora (0.073 vs 0.051 alpaca; 0.136 vs 0.109 dolly). A model learning from its own
samples beat distilling a weaker teacher's blind paraphrases.

**3. The Claude teacher remains the ceiling.** Ranking at p90: Claude (raw or distilled)
> self-distill > gpt-oss teacher > base policy. So data quality sets the ceiling; the
self-distillation procedure narrows but doesn't close the gap to a frontier teacher.

**4. The student ≈ the teacher.** `claude_rwr` retains ~92% of raw Claude's p90 OR
(0.150 vs 0.164) and actually edges it on mean/median/p75 (the body of the
distribution), at equal similarity — distillation broadened the signal even though the
teacher keeps a marginally heavier extreme tail. (Detail in `claude_training_results.md`.)

**5. It's all the similarity axis.** `llama_self_rwr` inherited the base policy's style:
the *highest refusal_delta of any trained model* (mean 2.58 alpaca, above baseline's
2.24) but *low similarity* (0.677). OR gates delta through `exp(5·(sim−0.75))`, so the
Claude models — high delta **and** high similarity (~0.87) — win. Self-distillation
lifted similarity over baseline (0.592→0.677) while keeping delta high, which is exactly
where its +46–76% came from.

**6. Mean vs p90 disagree.** On *mean*, `rwr_v3` lands last (below even `baseline`):
its distribution is peaky (decent tail, weak body), whereas `llama_self_rwr`/`baseline`
lift the whole distribution. Mean rewards broad consistency; p90 rewards the extreme
tail RWR trains on. The `rwr_mean_vs_p90_alpaca.png` panels show the swap.

## Self-distillation data + training detail

- Generation: 5000 prompts × 8 = 40,000 candidates; **39,952 scored pairs, 95.9%
  positive**, mean OR 0.0543. (For contrast, gpt-oss blind paraphrase was 41% positive,
  taskaware Claude+Gemini 91%.) The base Llama, when prompted, already shifts refusal in
  the intended direction on nearly all of its own samples.
- Training: v3 recipe (LoRA r=32, k=5.0, bins [0,0,0,1,16], 3 epochs). Per-epoch
  train_loss 0.68 → 0.31 → 0.18; **val_loss 1.26 → 1.50 → 1.56** — an overfitting
  signature (same pattern the v1-dolly retrain flagged). Eval used the epoch-3 `final`
  checkpoint; **epoch_1 may score higher on held-out** (untested — checkpoints saved at
  `~/scratch/rwr_llama_self_checkpoints/epoch_{1,2,3}` on <cluster>).

## Caveats

- **Proxy metric.** Activation-based OR, not behavioral refusals (Plan C unrun).
- **Single seed / single variant.** One eval_seed (99); raw-Claude arm is alpaca-only.
- **Generation-length asymmetry.** Raw Claude was capped at 256 new tokens vs the Llama
  arms' 64; this favors raw Claude slightly, yet it only edges `claude_rwr` at the tail.
